GRAMStack Integration Plan

How the playground, and any third party, uses GRAMStack for sign-in, consent and registry access. Consent is voice only on a phone call (IVR) and both text and voice in the playground app, and every step works on a call with no screen. The playground keeps the conversation; GRAMStack keeps identity, consent, grants and evidence.

The decision in four lines

Playground becomes a consumer

Like SakhiCredit, it registers a Hydra client and consumer certificate, creates one access request per question and fetches once with a grant. It stops reading registers directly in hosted mode.

Sign-in moves to eSignet

The didi picker and the shared access code are replaced by eSignet through GRAMStack. On a call, GRAMStack's voice channel answers the line and runs a new decoupled sign-in; VID and OTP are keyed, never spoken.

Consent fits the channel

GRAMStack's consent portal is web and English today. A new Hindi profile adds two modes: voice only for IVR calls, and text with voice in the playground app. Both bind what she heard or read, and how she answered, to the receipt.

Connectors become adapters

Department sources onboarded in the playground are published as GRAMStack adapters through one configurable Go adapter built on the SDK.

Target architecture

Authentication flow

GRAMStack separates three things the playground currently merges into "pick your name": proving who she is (eSignet), proving she owns a registry record (registry subject verification), and agreeing to a disclosure (consent). The playground should follow the same split, on both channels.

With a screen

Web or app

  1. She opens the playground. It creates a POST /v1/access-requests only when a question needs records, so sign-in can wait until then.
  2. The playground opens the gateway's interaction_url. AuthN sends her to eSignet (authorization code, PKCE, state, nonce).
  3. After the callback, the Gateway asks the registry adapter to verify that she owns the requested record. The adapter returns a verified context, not data.
  4. The consent page shows the notice; she decides; she returns to the playground.
  5. When the status says CONSENTED, the playground asks for a grant, fetches once and answers.

This is the SakhiCredit flow unchanged, so it can ship first. From phase 3 the page also speaks the notice.

Audio only

Phone call, no screen

  1. She calls one published number, which GRAMStack's voice channel answers. It forwards audio to the playground for the conversation. Caller ID is only a locator, never proof.
  2. When records are needed, the playground creates the access request and asks the channel for a protected segment with a signed, single-use token bound to the request. The channel cuts the playground's audio leg.
  3. The channel runs a decoupled sign-in through AuthN. She keys her VID; eSignet sends the OTP by SMS; she keys the six digits. Nothing secret is spoken. GRAMStack's Hydra evaluation already plans a gateway-run decoupled flow as its substitute for CIBA, for consent by link or SMS. This extends it to a call.
  4. The Gateway asks the adapter to verify that the record is hers, then creates the consent request. The channel speaks the notice and she confirms the read-back by pressing 1.
  5. Once the consent is active, the channel announces the handback and restores the playground's leg. The playground asks for a grant and fetches as on the web.

The playground never carries the audio of the protected segment, because it never holds the call. Sign-in on a call depends on eSignet agreeing to the decoupled flow; until then, phone calls can only ask questions that need no records.

The contract in detail

A consumer talks to GRAMStack through six routes. The reference connector reaches all six through the Python SDK; cancel is there for a changed scope. The examples use fictional identifiers.

RouteCalled withWhat it does
POST /oauth2/tokenThe consumer's client credentialsReturns a consumer token. It identifies the organisation, never her
POST /v1/access-requestsConsumer token, idempotency keyChecks and stores the request. Answers 202 with the request id, a status URL and an interaction URL
GET /v1/access-requests/{id}Token of the consumer that owns the requestThe current state and the next action. Polling is enough; no webhook is assumed
POST /v1/access-requests/{id}/cancelOwning consumer's tokenCancels authority that has not been used. Answers 409 once the retrieval was admitted
POST /v1/access-requests/{id}/grantOwning consumer's token, idempotency keyChecks the consent, the verified subject and the registry policy. Returns a grant or a safe refusal. A retry returns the same grant
GET /v1/access-requests/{id}/dataThe grant, over the consumer's client certificateThe one admitted retrieval. Evidence is committed before release. Never prefetched and never retried

GRAMStack takes the consumer's identity from its registered client, not from the body. Changing any field means cancelling and starting a new request with a fresh notice.

Consent by voice

GRAMStack's consent-portal profile 0.1.0 supports one presentation: English, on the web. Its architecture already describes a presentation record with channel VOICE, and leaves speech recognition and the reading of her answer to the product. That record is an extension contract. It is not in the published message schema, and it needs a versioned contract and capability negotiation before any client uses it on the wire. This plan goes further than that design: it moves the notice and the interpreter into a GRAMStack voice channel, so the receipt records what GRAMStack itself played and heard. The next section compares the two.

What the receipt binds

  • Channel VOICE, language hi, and the notice template version, rendered text and its digest.
  • Digest of the synthesised notice audio, with the TTS model and voice id.
  • The decision transcript, how it was matched (keypad, word list or model), the confidence, and the keypad confirmation after the read-back.
  • ASR model version and the custom word list version (for example "हओ" added for Sagar). Word list changes are reviewed by GRAMStack before use, since each added yes word also adds false yeses.
  • The eSignet authentication reference from the same call segment.
  • The decision clip (a few seconds) is kept encrypted for 90 days under GRAMStack's key, then deleted. Without it a transcript digest only proves what speech recognition produced, not what she said; the keypad press is the stronger evidence.

Receipts reach her by SMS as a short reference number, and by voice: "पिछली अनुमतियाँ सुनने के लिए 2 दबाएँ". History works the same way: she calls, signs in again and hears her approvals and refusals. She can stop an approval that has not been used. An approval that was used is spent; for that she can ask the playground to erase what it still holds.

Three ways to take consent on a call

GRAMStack's design and this plan differ on one point: who speaks the notice and who hears her answer. GRAMStack's consent architecture has the product present the platform's exact notice and send back a structured decision. This plan has GRAMStack hold the call. A third way sits between them. The choice is open, and it is GRAMStack's maintainers' to make as much as the playground's.

GRAMStack renders the exact notice and returns it with a presentation id and a digest. Didi speaks it, reads her answer and sends a decision bound to that notice.

What GRAMStack must build

Weak point

Used
Today's chat prototype, on the mock only. It is the direction GRAMStack's own design takes
B · Platform holds the call

GRAMStack answers the published number, cuts the playground's audio leg, speaks the notice and takes the keyed VID, the OTP and the decision itself.

Who hears her answer
GRAMStack, and Bhashini as its processor. The playground hears nothing
What the receipt can show
First-hand evidence: the notice audio, the transcript and the key press
What GRAMStack must build
A voice channel service, telephony, a processor agreement with Bhashini, and the decoupled sign-in

The playground hears the spoken answer. GRAMStack gets the confirmation directly
What the receipt can show
The product's claim, plus a confirmation the product could not have made up
What GRAMStack must build

Weak point

Used
The fallback for phase 3 if GRAMStack will not hold calls

Phase 3 can be built on any of the three, because it covers consent only and uses test identities. Phase 4 cannot be built on A the way the mock does it: a real VID and OTP must not pass through the product. So either B is agreed, or sign-in stays on eSignet's own page and calls keep to questions that need no records.

Adapters in the current architecture

The playground has a Connector interface (tables(), rows()) with Excel, Google Sheets and SQL implementations, plus a department setup that names the users table, key columns, table owners, field wording and purpose policy. GRAMStack has adapters: independently hosted services with a public manifest, subject verification and a protected retrieval pipeline. They connect in two directions.

Inbound · playground reads through GRAMStack

connectors/gramstack.py

  • A new Connector whose tables() lists approved registries and resources from adapter manifests, for example lokos / shg.membership.
  • rows() is called by gateway.fetch with the consent id. That consent now holds a GRAMStack request id, so the connector requests a grant and makes one retrieval.
  • The where filter is dropped: the adapter scopes rows to the verified subject.
  • Field names map through the department setup, so the planner and the Hindi wording keep working unchanged.
  • The received copy is encrypted per session, as SakhiCredit does, and deleted when the conversation ends or after 30 minutes, whichever comes first.
Outbound · playground sources become adapters

adapters/tabular (Go, SDK v0.1.0)

  • One configurable adapter, not one per register. It uses the SDK's subject verification, grant verifier, attempt store and retrieval handler, and supplies a provider that reads one table.
  • Configuration is exported from the playground's department setup: table, key column, field map and owner become a resource with fields and a verification method.
  • First resources: dar.household_income, vbg.wages, agristack.land, alongside LokOS's shg.membership.
  • It passes the SDK's CheckProvider, CheckDiscovery and CheckRetrieval before the gateway trusts it.
  • Rewriting the protected pipeline in Python is not planned: the SDK already carries the hard parts (atomic claim, evidence before release, no replay).

When things go wrong

The codes are the ones the reference connector and the trial routes raise today. The states are GRAMStack's terminal outcomes. The rule under all of them: a data call is never retried, and anything unclear is treated as spent.

The general line is माफ़ कीजिए, GRAMStack से बात नहीं हो पाई। with the reason shown as a note and a button to start again. In the target design the three OTP and member rows never reach the playground: the code is keyed to GRAMStack's voice channel, not said to Didi.

GRAMStack's own rules settle the hard cases. If evidence cannot be committed, nothing is released. If the response is cut off, the state is DELIVERY_UNKNOWN and nothing is sent again. A grant never becomes unused again after a timeout, and a stale event cannot bring a finished request back to life.

Configuration

The trial reads nine settings, named in server/gramstack_trial.py. Two are secrets. On a laptop they come from environment variables, or all nine are replaced by one path, GRAMSTACK_SDK_FIXTURE, to a config file written by the mock or by the demo-stack wrapper.

Two more settings have defaults and are not secret: GRAMSTACK_TRIAL_PURPOSE and GRAMSTACK_TRIAL_RETENTION. When a setting is missing, the trial card stays disabled and names what is missing. The local stacks make new certificates and credentials on every start, so the playground has to be restarted after them.

Changes by repository

Didi ka Playground
  • server/connectors/gramstack.py: consumer client (Hydra token, mTLS, idempotency keys, no automatic retries on data).
  • server/consent/ledger.py: becomes a cache of GRAMStack consent (request id, status, receipt reference), checked against GRAMStack before each use; local voice interpretation stays for mock mode only.
  • server/sources.py login: replaced by a sign-in step that returns an opaque principal reference. The didi picker remains behind PLAYGROUND_MODE=mock, and a hosted app refuses to start in mock mode or with the mode unset. The shared access code is retired.
  • Hosting: the consumer backend moves off Vercel serverless to a service with a KMS-backed signer for the Hydra secret and mTLS key; the static app can stay on Vercel.
  • Model host: Bhashini is named as the processor for speech and for Gemma 4. The writer prompt gets only the fields she approved.
  • Redaction: record values, VID, OTP, grants and receipt tokens are stripped before Langfuse and GlitchTip; a test fails if any reach them.
  • Telephony: the playground no longer answers calls. It exposes an audio stream endpoint that GRAMStack's channel connects to, and requests protected segments with a signed token.
  • After the fetch: delete the copy when the conversation ends, file a handling report deleted, and poll the private rights-case queue to answer erasure cases.
  • Department setup: "Export adapter descriptor" produces the tabular adapter's configuration.
  • Admin aggregate views stay on local mock data. Per-person consent cannot authorise totals across SHGs; that needs a department-level purpose GRAMStack does not define yet.
GRAMStack
  • specs/consent-voice/0.1.0: a versioned presentation contract with VOICE, TEXT_VOICE and TEXT modes, Hindi templates, the receipt fields and conformance checks listed above, and the capability negotiation that goes with it. It builds on the presentation record the consent architecture already sketches.
  • A handover contract for third parties (screen redirect, or a callback request that GRAMStack places from its published number), so any consumer reuses the consent channels. The consent mode is never taken from the request.
  • Voice channel service: answers the published number and holds every call (media anchor), streams audio to the consumer outside protected segments, speaks notices, collects keyed VID, OTP and confirmation, and posts decisions under service mTLS. Uses Bhashini ASR/TTS under a processor agreement; sends nothing to consumer tracing.
  • Decoupled sign-in in AuthN for calls. It extends the gateway-run decoupled flow that the Hydra evaluation plans as the CIBA substitute, is agreed with the eSignet operator, and caps OTP attempts per call, VID and caller number.
  • Sequential notices in one call segment for questions that need two registries. A grouped notice is a later spec proposal.
  • adapters/tabular on SDK v0.1.0, with conformance tests and fictional fixtures that match the playground's mock registers (Sunita D-001, Santosh D-005).
  • eSignet fixture identities for those didis, so the same people exist on both sides.
  • Post-disclosure by voice: history, withdrawal of an unused approval and rights-case intake on a call, on top of the profile's web routes. The recipient side, handling reports and the private queue, is in the profile already.

about 2 weeks · can start after phase 2

Limits to keep in view

  • Everything here uses fictional identities and mock registers. Real eSignet, LokOS or Aadhaar onboarding needs approved credentials and is outside this plan.
  • Bhashini hosts the model. Her question, the fields she approved and the answer text all reach it, as the playground's named processor. An earlier version of the dataflow matrix showed no record fields reaching Bhashini. That was wrong.
  • GRAMStack's own design has the product present the voice notice. The platform-held call in this plan is a proposal to GRAMStack, not something it has agreed.
  • OTP by SMS assumes she has the registered mobile on the call. On a shared phone, the person speaking may not be the didi; eSignet authentication remains the only proof.
  • A receipt or a DeDi commitment does not prove the playground deleted its copy. The handling report and the rights-case resolution are the playground's own statements.
  • The review that produced this revision found four design-level issues on the phone path (spoken OTP, Bhashini outside the boundary, a playground-held call, and browser-only eSignet). Each is addressed above; the remaining risks are listed in the security model.
  • Third parties get the same channels but not the playground's answers or agents; each registers, is approved and is named to her in the notice.
  • Credential wallet features stay off in hosted mode, as now, until the wallet's issuer is aligned with GRAMStack evidence.
